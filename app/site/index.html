witchbox1Witch house toys for the M-VAVE FM-1

Eighty small generative sequencers, each with four knobs and a four-digit seed, playing eight at a time on one clock and one run of chords. Limping garage beats, sub bass, detuned saws, glass arpeggios, screwed voices, record crackle; shift registers, Markov chains and cellular automata for drums; and conductors that cut the band, fog it, dub it out or move its key.

Eighty toys, four shelves

A toy is a tune-maker with a method: a limping beat, a walking bass, a chord that ducks on every kick, a bell that answers itself. The same seed and knobs play the same notes every time, here and on the Pico that Playbox runs on.

A band of eight

Eight seats, each a toy, on one clock and one run of chords. Each seat has its volume, a mute and a solo; the conductors sit in a seat like any toy and work on the others.

A Tape gives every seat a lane of bars, on or off, 4 to 64 bars long. A Clock gives every seat a ring of sixteen dots spread as evenly as they can be, so eight seats on different counts make a slowly turning song. REC held fills a band, or an arrangement, at random; the band page shows which seat the chords, the lead and the kick are coming from.

Sixteen slots keep toys, four keep whole bands with their arrangement, and the band is saved as you left it.

The keys are pads

The four knobs are always the toy's four. Everything else lives on the buttons and the twenty-seven keys.

White keys 1 to 7 held, steer the chord root; every toy follows, the chord that is coming included.

White keys 9 to 16 mute a seat; with ENV held, solo it. Key 8 brings everyone back in.

Black keys are dice and undo, half and double time, freeze, brake, filter, crush, drive, an echo throw, and calm.

HOME opens the shelf on the keys, twenty toys a page. REC rolls a new seed; held, a random band.

SEQ switches the arrangement: band, tape, clock. SEL steps through the dark scales.

Hold GLO for scale, chords, tempo and rate; EDIT for the seed's digits; FX for drive, crush, echo and feedback; ENV for the mixer; LFO for a slow drift on each knob.

Install, and the way back

witchbox1 replaces the FM-1's firmware; nothing of M-VAVE's stays. Installing is at your own risk. Version {{VERSION}}.

  1. Back up first. If the FM-1 already runs witchbox1, the backup page saves the band and every saved toy to a file.
  2. Install from Chrome or Edge with the FM-1 on a USB data cable, from the install page. Take the m-bootloader option: it replaces M-VAVE's bootloader with an open one that keeps a recovery menu (PLAY held while switching on) and an update mode (REC held), even when a firmware will not start. Do not unplug until it says Done; the FM-1 restarts by itself, twice with m-bootloader.
  3. If it will not start, witchbox1 comes up in a safe mode after two failed starts and says where it failed; USB works there, so the install page can reinstall or put M-VAVE's firmware back. With m-bootloader, PLAY held at power-on reaches the recovery menu whatever happened.
  4. Back to M-VAVE's firmware at any time: the install page's "Back to the stock firmware" writes M-VAVE's V15 (download it from m-vave.com first), and M-VAVE's own updater works too.

An earlier witchbox1 carried its own copy of the platform layer, and an interrupted update under it left an FM-1 needing a hardware recovery. This one is built on fm1-base, the maintained layer other FM-1 firmwares update through, with m-bootloader on offer so that a failed start is not the end.

For the command line: download {{PKG}} and run python3 base/tools/fm1_install.py {{PKG}} from the source tree.
